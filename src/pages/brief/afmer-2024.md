ATF's Annual Firearms Manufacturing and Export Report for 2024, the Final version, puts calendar year 2024 at 9,474,540 firearms manufactured and 399,902 exported. Both totals are the sum of ATF's five type totals. ATF prints no grand total. The report's data is as of January 30, 2026. This note reads AFMER 2024 Final, as published Jan 30, 2026. It isn't retail sales. It isn't NICS. It isn't excise tax dollars.

## What the report is

AFMER is ATF's annual count of what U.S. licensed manufacturers made and shipped. The 2024 Final covers calendar year 2024 activity. ATF's summary page says it was prepared January 30, 2026, with report data as of the same date.

The report has two parts. A one-page summary prints national totals by type. The pages after it list figures license by license.

It runs late. Calendar 2024 numbers carry a data date of January 30, 2026.

## What "manufactured" means

ATF defines the word on the summary page. "For purposes of this report only," manufactured means "firearms, including separate frames or receivers, actions or barreled actions, manufactured and disposed of in commerce during the calendar year."

Two things follow from that.

- Frames, receivers, actions and barreled actions count, if they were made and disposed of in commerce.
- Disposed of in commerce is the test. A gun built and still sitting in the maker's inventory is a different thing.

The summary page doesn't list other exclusions. We don't add any.

## How this sits next to NICS and FAET

There are three official series people reach for. They count three different things.

- **NICS** counts background checks. A check isn't a sale. Here's [why a background check is not a firearm sale](/nics-is-not-sales/).
- **FAET** counts tax dollars, at 10 or 11 percent of the maker's or importer's sale price. We cover that in [firearms and ammunition excise tax collections](/firearms-ammo-excise-tax-collections/).
- **AFMER** counts units U.S. makers reported as made and disposed of in commerce.

Checks, dollars, units. None of them is a count of guns sold at retail. Lining them up is fine. Swapping one for another isn't.

A license count is a fourth number, and it's not a production count either. See [how many FFLs are there](/how-many-ffls/).
