ATF's Annual Firearms Manufacturing and Export Report for 2024, the Final version, puts calendar year 2024 at 9,474,540 firearms manufactured and 399,902 exported. Both totals are the sum of ATF's five type totals, because ATF prints no grand total. The report's data is as of January 30, 2026, and this note reads AFMER 2024 Final, as published Jan 30, 2026. It counts what makers manufactured and disposed of in commerce, so it isn't retail sales, NICS checks or excise tax dollars.

## What the report is

AFMER is ATF's annual count of what U.S. licensed manufacturers made and disposed of in commerce, and the 2024 Final covers calendar year 2024 activity. ATF's summary page says it was prepared January 30, 2026, with report data as of the same date, so the report runs well behind the year it counts.

The report has two parts: a one-page summary prints national totals by type, and the pages after it list figures license by license.

## What "manufactured" means

ATF defines the word on the summary page. "For purposes of this report only," manufactured means "firearms, including separate frames or receivers, actions or barreled actions, manufactured and disposed of in commerce during the calendar year." Two things follow from that definition:

- Frames, receivers, actions and barreled actions count, if they were made and disposed of in commerce.
- Disposed of in commerce is the test. A gun built and still sitting in the maker's inventory is a different thing.

The summary page doesn't list other exclusions, and we don't add any.

## How this sits next to NICS and FAET

People reach for three official series, and they count three different things:

- **NICS** counts background checks, and a check isn't a sale; see [why a background check is not a firearm sale](/nics-is-not-sales/).
- **FAET** counts tax dollars, at 10 or 11 percent of the maker's or importer's sale price. We cover that in [firearms and ammunition excise tax collections](/firearms-ammo-excise-tax-collections/).
- **AFMER** counts units U.S. makers reported as made and disposed of in commerce.

None of the three (checks, dollars, units) is a count of guns sold at retail, so it's fine to line them up and a mistake to swap one for another.

A license count is a fourth number, and it isn't a production count either; see [how many FFLs are there](/how-many-ffls/).

## How we update

This note runs once a year, when ATF posts the next AFMER year, and we don't print a date ATF hasn't announced. If ATF revises a year, we keep both versions with their dates; that's [how we cite](/methodology/).
