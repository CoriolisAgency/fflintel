The FBI recorded 2,221,553 NICS firearm background checks in October 2026. That's down from 2,344,310 in October 2025, on a table the FBI created November 2, 2026. NSSF's adjusted figure for the same month is 1,402,422, in a release dated November 4, 2026. NSSF says that's up 7.9 percent from a year earlier. The two series moved in opposite directions. Neither one counts guns sold. The FBI says so on its table. NSSF says so in its release.

| Figure | October 2026 | October 2025 | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| FBI NICS checks, Month/Year table | 2,221,553 | 2,344,310 | FBI, file created Nov 2, 2026 |
| NSSF-adjusted NICS | 1,402,422 | 1,299,312 | NSSF release, Nov 4, 2026 |
| "Unadjusted FBI NICS figure," as NSSF prints it | 2,104,063 | 2,289,774 | NSSF release, Nov 4, 2026 |

## The FBI number, as printed

The FBI's Month/Year table covers January 1, 2020 through October 31, 2026. The PDF was created on November 2, 2026. The October 2026 cell reads 2,221,553.

Here's how that sits against nearby cells on the same table:

- October 2025: 2,344,310. October 2026 is 5.2 percent lower.
- September 2026: 2,000,629. October is 11.0 percent higher.
- January through October 2026: 21,652,247. That's the printed 2026 Grand Total, with November and December still blank.
- January through October 2025: 21,733,375. That's the sum of the ten printed 2025 monthly cells. 2026 is 0.4 percent lower.

The percents are our math from the FBI cells. The FBI table prints counts only. The same table puts the program total since 1998 at 548,899,543.

The note at the bottom of the FBI table says it plainly: "These statistics represent the number of firearm background checks initiated through the NICS. They do not represent the number of firearms sold. Based on varying state laws and purchase scenarios, a one-to-one correlation cannot be made between a firearm background check and a firearm sale."

## The NSSF-adjusted number

NSSF is the firearm industry trade association. It publishes an adjusted NICS figure each month. For October 2026 that figure is 1,402,422. NSSF says that's 7.9 percent above its October 2025 adjusted figure of 1,299,312.

NSSF explains the method in its own words. The adjusted data "were derived by subtracting out NICS purpose code permit checks and permit rechecks used by states for CCW permit application checks as well as checks on active CCW permit databases. NSSF started subtracting permit rechecks in February 2016."

This is a trade association's adjustment of FBI data. It's not a government statistic.

NSSF carries its own caveat. The release calls the adjusted figure "not a direct correlation to firearms sales." It also says the statistics "do not represent the number of firearms sold or sales dollars."

The release names states but prints no state counts:

- Top 5 for adjusted NICS checks: Texas, Florida, California, Pennsylvania, Virginia.
- Top 5 for FBI NICS handgun checks: Florida, Texas, Pennsylvania, California, Tennessee.
- Top 5 for FBI NICS long gun checks: Texas, Pennsylvania, Florida, California, Wisconsin.

October 2026 has two numbers that both get called the FBI total.

| FBI Month/Year table, October cell | 2,221,553 | FBI PDF, created Nov 2, 2026 |
| "Unadjusted October 2026 FBI NICS figure" | 2,104,063 | NSSF release, Nov 4, 2026 |

The year-ago comparison differs too. For October 2025, the FBI table prints 2,344,310. NSSF's release prints 2,289,774.

We haven't reconciled these from the FBI state file. We won't guess why they differ. If you cite October, cite each figure with its own source. Don't treat them as one number.

## Why a check is still not a sale

A NICS total counts checks. A lot of things make a check that isn't a new gun leaving a counter.

- Permit checks and permit rechecks run through NICS. NSSF subtracts them. The FBI total doesn't.
- One check can cover more than one firearm.
- A denied check is still a check.
- Dealers run checks on used guns too. NSSF's release says NICS checks sales or transfers of new or used firearms.

The count can also miss sales. NSSF says 28 states have at least one qualified alternative permit under the Brady Act. In those states a permit holder can buy from a dealer without a separate check for that transfer. NSSF doesn't add those transfers back.

So the FBI figure runs high for some reasons and low for others. NSSF's figure fixes one of those and says so. For the long version, read [why a background check is not a firearm sale](/nics-is-not-sales/). GunSearchEngine also lays out [NICS checks next to firearm sales](https://www.gunsearchengine.com/nics-vs-firearm-sales).

Thinking about ranking states from the FBI file? Read [how to read the state table](/nics-checks-by-state/) first. A raw state rank isn't a sales rank.
