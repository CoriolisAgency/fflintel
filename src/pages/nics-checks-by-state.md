The FBI's state table for 2026 runs through October 31, 2026, on a file modified November 2, and Illinois printed the most checks of any state in October at 213,360, with Texas at 169,586. Those are background checks, not firearm sales. Kentucky's October cell is 31,007, against 297,083 in October 2025, which is why a raw state rank, or a raw year-over-year by state, can mislead you before you ask what kind of check it was.

## Where the table comes from

The FBI publishes NICS Firearm Background Checks: Month/Year by State as one PDF with a page per year, and the copy we read was modified November 2, 2026.

- The Year 2026 page covers January 1 through October 31, 2026. November and December are blank.
- The Year 2025 page covers the full calendar year.

Each row is a state or territory, each column is a month, and the last column is the Grand Total.

Watch that last column, because it means different things on different pages: on the current-year page Grand Total is year-to-date, and on a past-year page it's twelve months. Illinois shows 2,301,632 for 2026 and 2,546,739 for 2025, so the first is ten months and the second is twelve; don't compare them as if they match.

The FBI's note sits on every page: "These statistics represent the number of firearm background checks initiated through the NICS. They do not represent the number of firearms sold. Based on varying state laws and purchase scenarios, a one-to-one correlation cannot be made between a firearm background check and a firearm sale."

These are the five largest October 2026 cells on the FBI state table, with the same states a year earlier; Kentucky is added for the lesson below.

| Texas | 169,586 | 147,277 | 1,410,586 | 1,739,790 |
| Florida | 122,508 | 118,752 | 987,440 | 1,258,156 |
| California | 116,855 | 139,097 | 1,165,723 | 1,455,424 |

We cover that gap in the [October 2026 note](/brief/nssf-adjusted-nics-october-2026/), and this page doesn't treat the two FBI files as already reconciled.

## Why a raw state rank misleads

Illinois sits high every month: its lowest 2026 month so far is February at 195,195, and its highest is June at 289,275. Kentucky swings, as its 2026 cells show:

| Month | Kentucky, 2026 |
|---|---:|
| January | 291,317 |
| February | 31,763 |
| March | 31,106 |
| April | 27,738 |
| May | 24,294 |
| June | 21,598 |
| July | 272,859 |
| August | 104,210 |
| September | 22,937 |
| October | 31,007 |
| Year to date | 858,829 |

2025 swung too, on a different calendar: Kentucky printed more than 290,000 checks in eight months of 2025 and fewer than 40,000 in the other four. October 2025 was one of the high months, and October 2026 is a low one.

So Kentucky's October looks like a collapse, 89.6 percent below October 2025 by our math, but the table counts checks and can't tell you that fewer guns sold. Some states run permit checks and permit rechecks through NICS, so a year-over-year in a state like that can be a calendar effect.

The same caution runs the other way: our math has Texas up 15.1 percent on October 2025 and Illinois up 8.2 percent, and those are changes in checks, too.

NSSF handles this at the national level with an adjusted figure that subtracts permit checks and permit rechecks, but it doesn't publish adjusted counts by state. Its November 4, 2026 release names the adjusted top five for October as Texas, Florida, California, Pennsylvania and Virginia. Illinois leads the raw table and isn't on that list, and that gap is the point of this page.

## What the table does not tell you

The table doesn't count sales, split new guns from used ones or report dollars. It also doesn't include transfers that skip a fresh check: NSSF says 28 states have at least one qualified alternative permit under the Brady Act, and in those states a permit holder can buy from a dealer without a separate check for that transfer.

There isn't an official state-by-state sales estimate built from this file, and we will not invent one. For the longer version, read [why a background check is not a firearm sale](/nics-is-not-sales/).

## Before you cite a state

Run these four checks before you quote any cell from the state table.

1. **Which page?** The current-year page is year to date. A past-year page is twelve months.
2. **Which month?** One month in a single state can swing by hundreds of thousands, as Kentucky's did, so look at the whole row before you quote a cell.
3. **Which kind of check?** This table doesn't split permit checks from gun checks. If that split matters, you need a different FBI file.
4. **Which claim?** "Illinois ran the most NICS checks in October 2026" is true on this table. "Illinois sold the most guns" isn't something this table can tell you.

## How we update this page

When the FBI posts the next state file, we refresh the table and change the "as of" line. Last month's cells don't get overwritten quietly; they move to a short log below, or into that month's national note. If the FBI revises a cell, we keep both figures, and that's [how we cite](/methodology/).
