## What FAET is, and the rates

FAET is the federal firearms and ammunition excise tax. TTB, the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, collects it.

The federal excise tax is owed by the manufacturer, producer or importer, and is paid when it first sells the firearm or ammunition ([26 U.S.C. 4181](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4181)). Dealers and buyers don't send it to the government. It's built into the price they pay. TTB covers the details on its [firearms and ammunition excise tax page](https://www.ttb.gov/regulated-commodities/firearms/taxes-and-tax-exemptions).

The rates, as TTB states them:

- 10 percent of the sale price of pistols and revolvers.
- 11 percent of the sale price of other firearms, and of shells and cartridges.

TTB cites 26 USC Section 4181(a) and 27 CFR Section 53.61(a).

Every TTB collections release we opened carries the same note: "This is an unofficial report. Official revenue collection figures are in the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau Annual Report."

People often search for this series as "Pittman-Robertson excise tax collections." This page covers the collections TTB reports. We haven't opened an official page today that states where the money goes, so we don't describe that here.

## How to read a TTB quarter

TTB works on the federal fiscal year. It starts October 1.

- "Q1 2026" on TTB's file is October 1 through December 31, 2025.
- "Q2 2026" is January 1 through March 31, 2026.

That's the most common misread. A fiscal Q1 is the prior calendar year's fourth quarter.

The tables are in thousands of dollars. $178,827 thousand is $178,827,000. We print TTB's figures as TTB prints them.

The comparison columns move. TTB updates prior periods when collections get adjusted. Its note says quarterly figures from earlier reports may not add up to later year-to-date figures. That's a revision, not an error.

## Why collections are not units, and not NICS

FAET is dollars of tax on the maker's or importer's sale price. Price moves it as well as volume. A quiet unit quarter can still post a firm dollar total. The reverse happens too.

That makes it a different series from the other two official numbers people reach for:

- NICS counts background checks. A check isn't a sale. Here's [why a background check is not a firearm sale](/nics-is-not-sales/).
- ATF's manufacturing report counts units made. We cover that in [how many guns U.S. makers built](/brief/afmer-2024/).

None of the three shows a brand what shoppers looked for. Tax dollars tell you what makers and importers sold, at their price, a quarter or more later. They don't tell you which gun a shopper searched for and couldn't find. That's a different kind of data. GunSearchEngine publishes [demand data aimed at firearms brands](https://www.gunsearchengine.com/oem-firearms-demand-analytics). There's also [brand-side search demand data from Coriolis](https://www.coriolisagency.com/demand-intelligence?utm_source=fflintel&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fflintel-firearms-ammo-excise-tax-collections).

## Why the lag

The newest TTB collections here end March 31, 2026, and were issued June 12, 2026. Today is November 10, 2026.

Tax returns run behind too. TTB's 2026 schedule shows the calendar-year return for July 1 through September 30, 2026 was due November 2, 2026. That's a return due date. It isn't the date TTB posts collections.

## How we update

When TTB posts Q3, Q4 or the FY2026 final on its Tax Collections page, we add the row and change the "as of" line. Printed cells stay. If TTB revises a comparison column, we note it next to the old figure. That's [how we cite](/methodology/).
